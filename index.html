      First time
      in Golden Gai?

      A few things worth knowing before you open your first door.

      Where is Golden Gai?

      About 5 minutes on foot from Shinjuku Station, East Exit.

      It sits in a corner of Kabukicho. From the East Exit, walk to Yasukuni-dori and cross to the Kuyakusho-dori side. Then follow the tree-lined Shiki-no-Michi promenade for about 80 meters. The alleys open up on your right.

      How many bars are there?

      Close to 300, each with its own personality.

      The area grew out of a postwar black market, and many of its Showa-era wooden buildings still stand. Two shopkeepers' associations share it: Shinjuku Golden Gai on the south side, with around 110 shops, and Shinjuku Sanko on the north side, with around 190.

      How much money should I bring?

      Every bar sets its own prices, so ask first.

      Tell the staff your budget when you sit down, or ask how much a drink and the seat will cost. Many bars add a per-person seating charge, often called a “charge” or “cover”. That is normal here, not a trick.

      How do I choose a bar?

      Go by the name and the look, then ask around.

      Pick a door whose name or style matches your taste. Once you are settled, ask the staff or the regulars which bar to try next. Following those recommendations is how most people find their favorite.

      Can we go in as a group?

      Two or three people works best.

      Most bars seat fewer than ten. For larger groups, check how many seats a bar has before you go. On busy nights you may be turned away simply because there is no room.

      Are there events for first-timers?

      Yes. Three bar-hopping festivals make it easy to try new places.

      • April

        Sakura Matsuri (Cherry Blossom Festival)

        Held every April since 2013. Participating bars drop their cover charge and serve drinks at a flat ¥500, with outdoor live music too.

        Organized by the Shinjuku Sanko association

      • Last Sunday of August

        Noryo Kanshasai (Summer Thank-You Festival)

        Started by volunteers and now run by the association as a thank-you to visitors. No cover charge and ¥500 drinks at participating bars, so it is a good night to explore beyond your usual spot.

        Organized by the Shinjuku Golden Gai association

      • Autumn

        Aki Matsuri (Autumn Festival)

        Part of the Shinjuku Machi Bar Week bar crawl since 2016, with an outdoor exhibition of old Shinjuku photographs curated by artist Vivienne Sato.

        Organized by the local town magazine J-G

      Dates and prices above come from the 2022 guide. Check each year's announcement before you plan around them.

      Why do the alley gates look so new?

      Both associations renewed their entrance signs recently.

      In December 2020, the Sanko side put up new gate signs designed with the Japanese retailer BEAMS. In March 2022, the Golden Gai side replaced its gate signs for the first time in about 60 years.
